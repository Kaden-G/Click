Ideas that click, and stick

Click

Bite-sized ideas from the books that shaped the last decade, plus short explainer clips. Every card ends with one thing to try, because an idea only clicks once you use it.

Feed

106 clicks: 50 from 10 books published 2016 to 2024, 20 that test common beliefs against the evidence, 20 things people commonly get wrong, from how government works to money and everyday tech, and 16 on writing deployment runbooks. Each card opens as a question: guess first, then reveal. Tap Review later and Click brings the idea back just as it starts to fade.

One book at a time feels easier, but Mixed builds stronger recall. That's interleaving.

Interview

A five-minute interview on any topic. Five questions: explain, apply, find the limits, connect two ideas, and handle a scenario. Then you get feedback on each answer, what to sustain, and what to improve.

How it works

  • Answer each question in two to four sentences. Typing it out is retrieval practice.
  • The clock starts when the first question appears. At 5:00, whatever you've answered gets reviewed.
  • Ideas you miss can go straight into your review queue, so feedback turns into practice.

How Click teaches

Built on Make It Stick by Peter Brown, Henry Roediger and Mark McDaniel (2014). The common thread: learning that feels effortful lasts longer than learning that feels smooth.

Scene Script v0: the grammar

Ten primitives cover most concepts. If you'd have to invent the numbers for a compare, use split instead. The script supplies numbers and labels; the renderer owns every coordinate, color and easing curve.

Rules every script follows

  • One idea per clip, and end with a recall beat so watching turns into practice.
  • 1 to 8 beats, 4 to 20 seconds each, under 120 seconds total.
  • say is both the narration and the captions, so write it to be heard.
  • Data holds numbers and labels only, never positions. Layout belongs to the renderer.
  • Anything not drawn to scale says so on screen.
  • Clips about a research finding set strength to Strong, Moderate or Weak, and the badge stays on screen the whole time.
  • A beat waits for the voice to finish, so long lines stretch the beat instead of getting cut off.
